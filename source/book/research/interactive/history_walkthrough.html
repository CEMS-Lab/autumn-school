1. Specify what can move, what is loaded, and what is unknown

The specimen has a prescribed notch from x = 0 to 20 mm at y = 20 mm. Its notch nodes keep damage d = 1. The left edge has zero horizontal displacement; opposite vertical tractions pull the top and bottom edges apart. Vertical motion follows the applied tractions in this dynamic setup. Initial displacement and velocity are zero.

The recorded run uses a cosine loading ramp over 30 microseconds. With t the prescribed time, its load factor is

Where is the switch? The schedule changes formula when the ramp finishes, but the value and first time derivative join continuously. Moreover, time and loading are fixed inputs in particle-location recovery. A fixed boundary mask replaces constrained entries with their prescribed, parameter-independent values. If loading or moving boundaries were themselves unknown, those dependencies would need to enter the derivative too.

The particle centre c is different: it is an inverse parameter. A hard inside/outside material label would change when an element crossed the particle boundary. We use a smooth transition on the fixed mesh instead:

χ(x;c)=σ(r-‖x-c‖2+δεm)

Here x is an element centroid, r the radius, and epsilon_m the interface width. A tiny positive delta regularises the distance at the centre. Material fields interpolate between matrix and particle values using chi. The retained reference has c = (28, 23) mm, r = 3 mm, epsilon_m = 0.5 mm and a stiffness ratio of 2. Its fracture-energy ratio is 3. This sigmoid smooths the material interface. It is separate from the history sigmoid below.

2. Advance the displacement under the applied load

Displacement u tells us how each mesh node moves; v and a are its velocity and acceleration. The explicit predictor uses the previous state:

Prescribed displacements are then applied. Internal forces depend on the predicted displacement, the material fields and the previous damage. Mass, applied forces and the configured damping determine acceleration; a velocity correction completes the mechanics step. Schematically,

Man+1=fext-fint-fdamp

What makes this differentiable? Tensor arithmetic carries the dependence on material and earlier states. The fixed boundary replacement routes derivatives only through free entries when prescribed values are parameter-independent. The fixed support retains its exact prescribed constraint. Force evaluation still contains the constitutive choices discussed next, and a stable time step remains necessary.

3. Separate tensile crack-driving energy from compression

The finite-element strain is a linear function of displacement on this fixed mesh: epsilon = B u. The spectral split resolves principal strains, then keeps their positive parts. Stretching can drive opening damage; compression is treated through a separate constitutive contribution.

⟨q⟩+=max(0,q),d⟨q⟩+dq={0q<01q>0

Where is the kink? The positive-part operation changes slope at zero strain. Squaring it, as in a tensile-energy contribution, makes that scalar contribution continuously first differentiable; its second derivative still changes. The composed quantity can therefore be smoother than its constituent primitive.

The inspected two-dimensional spectral routine obtains principal strains from a trace and a square-root expression with a small positive regularisation inside the root. Positive parts use the executed clamp rule. This handles a local algebraic degeneracy. Stress tangents and complete fracture trajectories need their own smoothness analysis; the history sigmoid belongs to a separate operator.

4. Ask whether this material point has reached a new energy maximum

The current tensile energy psi-plus can fall during unloading or redistribution. The history H retains the largest value seen so far:

Hn+1=max(Hn,ψn+1+)

This is the decision in the playground. Below the old peak, the forward value keeps H_n. Above it, current energy takes over. The two values meet continuously at equality, but the selected slope changes. Calling the maximum a discontinuous step function would confuse its value with its derivative.

For an exact hard-branch derivative away from equality, the reverse weights for old history and current energy are (1, 0) or (0, 1). The retained run identifies the custom_subgrad history option: its forward still takes the hard maximum, while its backward uses sigmoid weights,

wψ=σ[s(ψ+-Hn)],wH=1-wψ

These weights define a surrogate derivative near the switch. The exact hard-map derivative selects the active branch away from equality. The inspected operator calls torch.sigmoid and saves its two inputs and the scale for its vector-Jacobian product. Its saved inputs suffice for this local product. For two inputs, these weights are also mathematically equivalent to a two-entry softmax. The important distinction is that this option keeps the hard forward value; the separate log-sum-exp option smooths that value. 5. Solve for a spatially regularised damage field

History supplies the crack-driving input to the AT2 damage equation. Fracture energy G_c resists damage, and length scale ell_0 controls its spatial regularisation. On the mesh, the unconstrained linear subproblem can be written

A(H,Gc,ℓ0)d~=b(H,Gc,ℓ0)

A contains local reaction and spatial-gradient terms; b contains the driving source. Prescribed notch values enter the reduced free-node system. The configured material corrections belong in A and b too. The linear solve itself is smooth locally when its reduced operator is invertible. Finite iterative-solver accuracy is a numerical issue, rather than a physical discontinuity.

Instead of recording every conjugate-gradient iteration, an implicit backward solves a transposed system. Let J measure the mismatch with observations, as defined in step 8. With its incoming sensitivity g and an auxiliary adjoint variable lambda,

ATλ=g,∂J∂q=λT(bq-Aqd~)

This is the contribution through the damage solve for an input q. Both the operator and the right-hand side change with inputs. The inspected field-parameter backward reconstructs the unconstrained state for this product. Measured primal, reconstruction and adjoint residuals establish the achieved accuracy of this equation-level derivative.

6. Enforce irreversibility and the physical bounds

Damage cannot heal in this formulation, and its value stays between zero and one. In the solve-then-project route, each free node uses

dn+1=min(1,max(dn,d~))

This introduces another switch, separate from history. A strictly interior node sends its incoming derivative into the unconstrained solve. A strictly lower-active node sends it into previous damage. A strictly upper-active node sends none into either input when the upper bound is the constant 1. At equality, the implemented tie convention matters. Fixed notch nodes remain pinned.

For example, previous damage 0.4 and a trial value 0.2 give final damage 0.4: changing that trial slightly has no effect, but changing the previous damage does. A trial value 0.7 routes the derivative through the solve. A trial value 1.2 gives 1 and a locally zero slope with respect to that trial.

The backward masks these routes and solves for the appropriate incoming right-hand side. Its operator derivative uses the pre-projection state from the linear solve. This derivation describes a solve-then-project map; a projected iterative constrained solve needs its own consistent derivative. These bound switches remain distinct from the history sigmoid.

7. Repeat: today’s damage changes tomorrow’s forces

The next mechanics step uses the updated damage, so the local stiffness and load redistribution change. Energy is recomputed, history compares against its old maximum, and damage is solved again. The crack path emerges from this coupled evolution and is displayed from the saved damage fields.

zn+1=Fn(zn,θ),z=(u,v,a,H,d)

The displayed run has 1,600 forward steps and 12 saved damage frames. The same recurrence applies at every one of these steps. The load is evaluated at the loop's prescribed time before each update. A frame labelled n is the state after update n.

The same node can change its history branch or damage-bound category during this sequence. A small parameter perturbation can also select a competing crack path. A local derivative follows the executed trajectory; alternative paths require separate forward evaluations. Phase-field regularisation spreads the crack in space, while the parameter-to-crack map can remain branch-dependent.

Connect each derivative rule to its diagnostic

We use a fixed-mesh smooth material field for particle motion, differentiate tensor mechanics operations, apply an explicit history reverse rule, and account for the damage solve and its bounds. These steps define an inverse calculation with explicitly identified smooth operators, branches and surrogate rules.

  • Classical local derivatives: test on a fixed, resolved branch using a finite-difference step-size sweep and measured solve residuals.
  • Selected conventions or surrogates: name the forward and backward rules separately and preserve any hard-forward FD disagreement.
  • Branch changes: inspect one-sided differences and which history or bound memberships changed. One-sided slopes distinguish branch selection from a derivative-implementation error.
  • Useful inverse information: even a correct gradient can be small when the observed crack scarcely responds to a distant particle. Additional observations answer a different question from derivative correctness.
