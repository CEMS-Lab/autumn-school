1.2.1. Branch Selection and Energy-Based Objectives in Physics-Informed Learning#

Learning Objective: In physical systems with multi-valued equilibrium solutions (such as post-buckling or symmetric crack paths), understand why standard supervised regression converges to unphysical conditional averages, and how physics-informed energy minimization guides models toward valid physical branches.

Download Practice Notebook Download with Worked Solutions Environment Setup

Consider a scalar physical system governed by a double-well potential energy. For a control parameter \in [0.25, 1]$, define the potential energy:

28488 W(q; s) = rac{(q^2 - s)^2}{4}. 28488

For any control value > 0$, the system admits two zero-energy equilibrium states:

28488 q_+(s) = +\sqrt{s} \quad ext{and} \quad q_-(s) = -\sqrt{s}. 28488

In this tutorial, we contrast two learning paradigms:

  1. Supervised Regression (Mean Squared Error): Trained on balanced observations from both branches.

  2. Physics-Informed Energy Minimization: Guided directly by the potential energy functional with a positive branch parameterisation ( = \operatorname{softplus}(a) > 0$).

1.2.1.1. Balanced supervision versus an energy objective#

The training inputs are repeated so that every \(s\) has one positive and one negative target. Under squared error, the best single deterministic prediction is their conditional mean, \(0\). That point is between the two reference branches and has nonzero residual \(q^2-s=-s\).

s_unique = torch.linspace(0.25, 1.00, 96, dtype=dtype).unsqueeze(1)
branch_positive = torch.sqrt(s_unique)
branch_negative = -branch_positive
s_supervised = s_unique.repeat_interleave(2, dim=0)
q_supervised_target = torch.stack(
    (branch_positive.squeeze(1), branch_negative.squeeze(1)), dim=1
).reshape(-1, 1)
s_heldout = torch.linspace(0.255, 0.995, 73, dtype=dtype).unsqueeze(1)

def residual(q, s):
    return q.square() - s

def energy(q, s):
    return residual(q, s).square() / 4.0

class TinyScalarMLP(nn.Module):
    def __init__(self, positive_branch=False):
        super().__init__()
        self.hidden = nn.Sequential(
            nn.Linear(1, 16),
            nn.Tanh(),
            nn.Linear(16, 16),
            nn.Tanh(),
            nn.Linear(16, 1),
        )
        self.positive_branch = positive_branch

    def forward(self, s):
        raw = self.hidden(s)
        return F.softplus(raw) + 1.0e-8 if self.positive_branch else raw

1.2.1.2. Stationary points and reference branches#

The energy derivative is

\[ \frac{\partial W}{\partial q}=q(q^2-s). \]

Thus \(q=0\) has zero energy gradient, a residual of \(-s\) and positive energy when \(s>0\). The next cell evaluates that fact directly. The energy model starts on the positive branch through its softplus output. This explicit branch selection determines which solution it learns.

s_stationary = torch.tensor(0.64, dtype=dtype)

1.2.1.3. Physical Interpretation#

The results highlight a fundamental distinction between standard data regression and physical energy minimization:

  • Supervised Regression: Under a squared-error loss, the optimal point prediction for equally balanced target branches is their conditional expectation, \(\mathbb{E}[q|s] = 0\). Although this minimises the statistical MSE loss, =0\( is not a physical equilibrium state—it has high potential energy (0; s) = s^2/4\) and a non-zero residual ^2 - s = -s$.

  • Energy-Based Minimization: By directly minimizing the physical potential energy (q; s)\( with a positive branch parameterization ( = \operatorname{softplus}(a)\)), the model converges directly to the positive physical equilibrium branch = +\sqrt{s}$, achieving near-zero energy and near-zero residual.

In physical modeling, predicting the conditional average of two valid configurations (such as an uncracked state between two symmetric crack paths) is often unphysical. Incorporating physical energy functions or branch conditioning ensures that learned models produce physically admissible states.

1.2.1.4.1. Exercise 1: Conditional Mean vs. Physical Branch Minimization#

At a fixed control parameter > 0\(, let = \sqrt{s}\). Suppose the training data contains equally balanced observations from both branches, 0\( and 569Xa\).

  1. Prove analytically that the standard Mean Squared Error loss is minimised uniquely at = 0$.

  2. For = 0.64\(, evaluate the residual = q^2 - s\), the energy \(, and the energy gradient \)\partial W/\partial q\( at = 0\).

  3. Discuss why gradient-based optimization on the energy potential requires breaking symmetry to escape the stationary point at = 0$.

Hint 1

Average the two squared errors before differentiating. For the energy, use \(\partial W/\partial q=q(q^2-s)\).

Worked solution 1

  1. The balanced squared-error objective is

\[ L(q)=\frac{1}{2}\left[(q-a)^2+(q+a)^2\right]=q^2+a^2. \]

Thus \(\partial L/\partial q=2q\), and its unique minimiser is \(q=0\). For \(s>0\), this lies between the two reference branches \(\pm\sqrt{s}\) and is their conditional mean.

  1. With \(s=0.64\) and \(q=0\),

\[ r=-0.64, \qquad W=\frac{(-0.64)^2}{4}=0.1024, \qquad \frac{\partial W}{\partial q}=0(-0.64)=0. \]

These values agree with the notebook output.

  1. The residual and energy are nonzero, and the quartic energy has a stationary point at \(q=0\). A gradient-based energy objective therefore gives a zero update at that exact point. The positive-branch model avoids this particular start by imposing \(q=\operatorname{softplus}(a)>0\).

1.2.1.4.2. Exercise 2: Physical Significance of Multi-Valued Predictions#

  1. Compare the validation metrics between the supervised regression model and the energy-minimized model. Which model accurately tracks the positive equilibrium branch, and what physical metric confirms this?

  2. In a continuum mechanics setting (such as crack propagation or structural buckling), what is the physical consequence if a neural network predicts the average of two bifurcated branches?

  3. How can a deep learning architecture be formulated if we want to represent both physical branches simultaneously without averaging them?

Hint 2

A zero-energy branch satisfies \(q^2-s=0\). Compare the residual and energy before interpreting the sign or model class.

Worked solution 2

  1. The energy-minimized model accurately tracks the positive equilibrium branch ( = +\sqrt{s}\(). Its root mean squared error against the positive branch is on the order of ^{-3}\), and its residual ^2 - s pprox 0\(. In contrast, the supervised regression model converges to pprox 0\), which incurs a large residual pprox -s$.

  2. In mechanics, predicting the average of two distinct equilibrium paths is typically physically non-viable. For example, if a plate can buckle either forward or backward, the average displacement is zero (flat plate), which is an unstable equilibrium that does not satisfy post-buckling load conditions. Similarly, averaging two symmetric crack branches would predict a non-physical damaged zone down the center where stresses are zero.

  3. To represent both branches simultaneously without averaging them, one can employ:

    • Mixture Density Networks (MDNs): Modeling the output as a conditional probability distribution with multiple Gaussian components.

    • Branch Conditioning / Latent Variables: Conditioning the network on a categorical branch selector \in {+1, -1}$.

    • Diffusion or Generative Models: Sampling from the multi-modal distribution of valid physical states.
